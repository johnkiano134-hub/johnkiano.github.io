Drugs take more than they give.

Know the real risks, spot the warning signs, and reach someone who can help. Recovery is possible, and it starts with one conversation.

What drugs do to a life

Harm rarely stays in the body alone. It spreads into family, school, work, and the whole community.

Addiction

Repeated use changes how the brain handles reward and stress. Stopping becomes painful, and cravings can last long after use ends.

Overdose

Strong opioids, mixed substances, and pills of unknown strength can stop breathing. Fake pills are a growing danger.

Mental health

Stimulants, cannabis, and hallucinogens can trigger anxiety, paranoia, and psychosis, especially in young people.

Family and future

Lost school years, lost jobs, broken trust, legal trouble, and unsafe homes for children are common costs.

Infection and injury

Shared needles spread HIV and hepatitis. Impaired driving and violence add to the toll.

Mixing substances

Alcohol with sedatives or opioids multiplies the danger. Many deaths involve more than one drug.

Warning signs to notice

One sign alone proves nothing. A pattern of several, lasting weeks, deserves a caring conversation.

  • Sudden changes in mood, sleep, or appetite
  • Pulling away from family and old friends
  • Falling grades or missed work
  • Money going missing or constant requests for cash
  • Secrecy about where they go and who they see
  • Bloodshot eyes, weight change, or neglecting hygiene
  • Pipes, pills, needles, or strange smells

Approach with calm and concern, not accusation. Say what you have seen, say you care, and offer to help them find support.

Find help today

Free, confidential support is available. You do not have to be sure, or ready, to make the call.
